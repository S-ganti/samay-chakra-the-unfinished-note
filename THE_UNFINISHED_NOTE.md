# Samay Chakra: The Unfinished Note

## The promise

You are carrying a melody learned from someone who is gone. You believe that finding its last note will let you hear them again. Across eight prahars, you discover that the melody was never meant to be finished. Its pauses are where other lives enter.

The player is never assigned a fixed relationship to the absent person. They might have been a parent, lover, sibling, friend or teacher. Small specific memories make the relationship intimate without deciding it for the player. The world is not an afterlife with a literal resurrection at the end.

## What makes it a game

Walk, double jump and fly through short spaces. Three listening circles in each place hold a phrase. Hold E near a circle: its arc fills, a sustained tone sounds, mist and sunbeams respond, and a memory appears. You can hear it with sound off through the visible resonance. A listening book keeps every phrase you discover. Discovery is optional; no emotional interpretation is graded and no memory is consumed.

In the final place, standing still on a surface for six seconds reveals another response. Singing is no longer the only way to act. This silence is presented as one moment of openness, not a cure for grief or a universal religious claim.

The implemented loop is: arrive, notice, approach, listen, receive an echo, move on or remain. There are 24 authored fragments. Flight is unlimited, failure returns you gently to the path, and the world remains available for revisits.

## Eight movements

| Prahar | Dramatic question | Environmental response | Musical idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enter the Ring / Life | Who taught you to listen? | Warm shafts slowly open, pollen rises, branches move in a separate depth plane. | A sparse phrase that stops before its expected resolution. |
| The Gathering / Love | Can two voices remain different and still belong together? | Water shivers below empty arcades; small warm motes carry the sense of departed company. | A second voice suggests a shared harmony. |
| Eclipse / Loss | What do you do when the answer never comes? | Cold fog, slow magenta moths, wet reflections. | The absent response matters as much as the audible note. |
| Brahma Muhurta / Grief | Can you stay beside an absence without filling it? | Very slow mist and water refraction; narrow points of light. | Long tones and wide intervals of quiet. |
| Diamond Ring / Joy | Is joy a betrayal of the person you miss? | Petals and dawn light brighten when you listen. | A new note enters the inherited phrase. |
| Dispersal / Daily life | How does love survive an ordinary morning? | Seed-like motes and moving foreground leaves frame a familiar street. | A melody becomes something a stranger might hum. |
| Zero Shadow / Inquiry | Who owns a song that keeps changing? | Sparse particles, bleached light, restrained motion. | A sound without an authoritative final cadence. |
| Return / Release | Can something remain unfinished and still be whole? | Honey shafts, drifting leaves, a final response to stillness. | A pause is left open for the world. |

The prahar/raga associations remain the user's original framework. The synthetic audio is an original sound sketch and should not be described as an authentic performance of the named ragas.

## Full-production direction

The next major musical feature should be a companion phrase that learns the player's timing in the love chapter and disappears in the loss chapter. Its missing rests should remain in the visual world, making absence playable without another explanatory paragraph. The prototype currently implements resonant tones and narrative fragments; it does not yet learn or replay player timing.

Likewise, a production version should let remembered notes alter paths, roots, bridges and the ring itself. Current interaction changes sound, light, ripple and lore state; platform geometry stays stable. Keeping these boundaries explicit prevents a written concept from being mistaken for implemented behavior.

The ending should resist a completion checklist. A future longer version can carry the player's phrasing and chosen silences into a final composition, rather than award a score or label one response enlightened. Nirvana is a creative question about grasping and openness here, not a doctrinal teaching. Cultural and musical consultation belongs in the final writing and score process.

## Visual approach and references

GRIS informs restraint, painterly architecture and the relationship of movement to feeling: https://nomada.studio/gris-game/

Journey informs wordless scale and emotional connection through play: https://thatgamecompany.com/journey/

Spiritfarer informs attention to ordinary care and farewell: https://thunderlotusgames.com/games/spiritfarer/

No artwork, music, character designs or story passages are extracted from these games.

The supplied concept website was read from its public HTML and JavaScript source at https://s-ganti.github.io/samay-chakra/. Its eight-prahar clock, generative music, beat-linked crowd motion, pigment controls, listen mode and user-track crossfades establish a musical installation. This game develops that basis into a personal traversal and discovery narrative. The site was source-reviewed; a rendered visual comparison was not completed.

## Current art pipeline

The original paintings remain intact under art/. Clean background editions under art/clean/ remove the painted traveler, scarf, shadows/reflections and other human silhouettes. The game loads these clean editions; the art viewer and original artbook preserve the first concept pass. Built-in image generation performs these edits, with the original compositions retained as references.

Water regions are refracted in narrow sampled strips while stone stays fixed. Mist, sun shafts, particles and near branches move independently. These effects make the backdrop active but do not turn flattened paintings into fully occlusion-correct multilayer scenery. Production architecture/foliage passes and animated foliage meshes remain a further visual upgrade.
